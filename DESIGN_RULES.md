# Abaad Design Rules

> Status: v0.1 — lessons established from the first real design test. These rules should evolve from reviewed work, not theory alone.

## 1. Posters are advertisements, not dashboards

Abaad social posts must be art-directed marketing compositions.

Product data, metrics, charts, cards, timestamps, status indicators, and UI may be useful visual material, but simply arranging them into a clean interface is not enough.

**Rule:** Product data may inspire the visual, but the poster must have a clear visual idea and an intentionally art-directed composition. Never default to `headline + KPI + chart + utility labels` as if designing a dashboard.

When product information appears, transform it into communication: decide what the audience should feel or understand immediately, then make that idea visually dominant.

## 2. Concept before layout

A different arrangement is not automatically a different concept.

Before drawing, every proposed direction must state its **Visual Idea** in one sentence. The idea should explain why the composition exists, not merely where elements are placed.

For example, a sales message could become:

- the sales number itself becomes the oversized visual hero;
- real Abaad product UI becomes the hero and the marketing message emerges from it;
- time/progression becomes the visual metaphor for "لحظة بلحظة".

These are different ideas. Moving the same headline, KPI, and chart into three layouts is not.

## 3. One dominant visual hero

Every poster needs a clear hero.

Possible heroes include:

- a powerful headline;
- one oversized number;
- one product screenshot or UI detail;
- one object or 3D form;
- one photograph;
- one visual metaphor.

Do not make several medium-strength elements compete equally. A viewer should know what to look at first within roughly two seconds.

## 4. Whitespace must have a job

Abaad values generous whitespace, but empty space alone does not make a design premium or minimal.

Whitespace should create at least one of these:

- focus;
- tension;
- hierarchy;
- rhythm;
- separation;
- balance;
- scale.

Large unused zones with no compositional purpose are not desirable. Judge whitespace by the relationships it creates between elements, not by how much empty canvas remains.

## 5. Avoid the generic SaaS-dashboard look

Do not automatically introduce interface conventions such as:

- `LIVE` badges;
- fake timestamps;
- monitoring status dots;
- generic bar charts;
- dashboard labels;
- fabricated analytics widgets.

Use them only when they are essential to the marketing idea or come from authentic Abaad product UI.

A social poster should not look like a screenshot of a monitoring dashboard unless that is explicitly the concept.

## 6. Product truth over invented UI

When the concept needs product UI, prefer real Abaad interface assets.

Do not fabricate realistic-looking product screens, statistics, feature states, or operational information that could be mistaken for the actual product.

If real UI is unavailable, either:

1. use an abstract/conceptual treatment that clearly reads as graphic communication, or
2. choose a different visual idea that does not depend on fake product UI.

## 7. Black and white lead; accent supports

Black and white remain the Abaad foundation.

Accent colors are selected because the concept needs them. An accent must not become an accidental permanent brand color through repetition.

Ask before using an accent:

**What job is this color doing?**

Good answers include highlighting growth, separating a key phrase, indicating a state, creating campaign character, or directing attention.

"It makes the design less empty" is not a sufficient reason.

## 8. Typography should behave as imagery

IBM Plex typography is part of the visual identity, not merely text placed on top of a layout.

For typography-led posters, explore:

- extreme but controlled scale;
- line breaks with purpose;
- contrast between weights;
- interaction between Arabic words and numbers;
- cropping or edge relationships when intentional and readable;
- asymmetric composition;
- selective accent treatment.

Do not assume that centering a large bold headline automatically creates strong art direction.

## 9. Social-first composition

The design must work first as a social-media creative, not as a web interface.

At mobile viewing size:

- the core idea should be understood quickly;
- the hero should remain visually dominant;
- small utility text should not carry important meaning;
- unnecessary micro-information should be removed;
- the composition should have a recognizable silhouette and hierarchy.

## 10. Controlled creativity

Abaad should be consistent without becoming repetitive.

Consistency should come from the brand system, typography, logo discipline, visual restraint, hierarchy, and quality — not from repeating the same poster template.

Different briefs may legitimately use typography, real UI, photography, illustration, 3D, data, or graphic forms. Choose the medium that produces the strongest idea.

## 11. Creative review gate

Before building a concept, reject it if any of the following is true:

- it can only be described as a layout rather than an idea;
- it resembles a dashboard more than an advertisement without a deliberate reason;
- there is no single visual hero;
- whitespace is large but compositionally inactive;
- the concept depends on fabricated product UI;
- accent color has no semantic or compositional purpose;
- it feels like a generic SaaS social template;
- changing another brand's logo to Abaad would make the design indistinguishable from that brand.

## 12. Lesson from Test 01

The first test was clean, readable, restrained, and technically competent. It successfully demonstrated logo usage, headline clarity, a strong KPI, and a low-clutter black/white foundation.

However, its overall structure behaved too much like a monitoring dashboard: utility status, timestamp, KPI, and a basic bar chart were arranged as interface information rather than transformed into a strong advertising idea. The upper whitespace was generous but insufficiently active, and the composition lacked a distinctive visual concept.

**Permanent lesson:** Clean execution is the baseline, not the creative goal. Abaad design should combine restraint with a memorable visual idea.
