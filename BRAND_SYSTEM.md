# Abaad Brand System

> Status: Foundation v0.1 — this document is intentionally incomplete and will evolve through approved design work.

## 1. Brand Direction

Abaad should feel modern, confident, clear, intelligent, and highly considered.

The visual system should favor strong ideas and strong composition over decoration. Designs should feel simple at first glance while still being distinctive and art-directed.

Abaad may learn from high-quality brands in adjacent spaces — including references such as Marn and Drahim — for principles such as typography, whitespace, product presentation, UI-led storytelling, 3D/product visualization, and campaign composition. References are inspiration for design thinking, not templates to copy. Every output must ultimately feel like Abaad.

## 2. Logo System

### The A symbol

The Abaad symbol is the brand's stylized **A**.

It has two simultaneous roles:

1. **Brand icon / symbol** — it may appear independently as the recognizable Abaad mark.
2. **The letter A in Abaad** — in the official brand lockup, the symbol itself represents the letter `A`, followed by `baad`.

Conceptually:

`[Abaad A Symbol] + baad = Abaad`

Do not add a normal Latin `A` next to `baad` when constructing the official brand lockup. The symbol is the A.

### Logo usage

- Primary logo treatment: black on a white or very light background.
- Reverse treatment: white on black or sufficiently dark backgrounds.
- The A symbol may be used independently as an icon, avatar, signature, watermark, or intentional graphic brand element.
- Preserve the original proportions and geometry of the mark.
- Do not redraw, distort, stretch, rotate, outline, or casually modify the mark.
- Do not add shadows, gradients, glows, strokes, or effects directly to the logo unless a future approved brand rule explicitly permits a specific treatment.

## 3. Color Philosophy

### Foundation

Abaad's core visual foundation is:

- **Black**
- **White**

Black and white should carry the identity and provide consistency across campaigns.

### Accent colors

Other colors are flexible **accent colors**, not mandatory dominant brand colors.

Accent colors may change according to the message, campaign, product feature, category, season, visual concept, or information hierarchy.

Examples may include blue, green, yellow, orange, pink, purple, or other intentional colors.

Rules:

- Begin from a black/white foundation unless the creative concept gives a strong reason not to.
- Use accent color intentionally to create emphasis, meaning, hierarchy, or campaign character.
- Avoid unnecessary rainbow palettes or color for decoration alone.
- A campaign may temporarily lean heavily into one accent color while retaining recognizable Abaad typography, composition, logo behavior, and visual discipline.
- Exact accent palettes will be documented later from approved work rather than invented prematurely.

## 4. Typography

Abaad's official design type family is **IBM Plex**.

### Arabic

Use:

`IBM Plex Sans Arabic`

### Latin and numbers

Use:

`IBM Plex Sans`

### Typography principles

- Typography is a primary visual element, not an afterthought.
- Strong headlines may carry an entire composition.
- Use weight, scale, whitespace, and alignment to establish hierarchy before adding decorative elements.
- Prefer Bold / SemiBold for major headlines when appropriate.
- Prefer Regular / Medium for supporting copy and information.
- Avoid introducing unrelated font families without explicit approval.
- Arabic-first compositions must respect RTL layout and natural Arabic reading rhythm.
- Latin text and numbers should integrate cleanly with Arabic rather than appear visually detached.

Exact type scales, line heights, tracking rules, and responsive poster typography will be established through real approved designs.

## 5. Visual Language

Abaad is not restricted to one fixed poster template.

The system should support multiple visual approaches while preserving a common design intelligence:

- Typography-led compositions
- Product UI and real interface screenshots
- Cards and structured information
- Product or feature visualization
- 3D elements when they strengthen the idea
- Photography when appropriate
- Illustrations and graphic forms when appropriate
- Data, metrics, charts, and operational information
- Strong use of negative space

The medium should follow the idea. Do not force 3D, photography, UI screenshots, illustration, or decorative graphics into every design.

## 6. Creative Principle

Abaad should not become a collection of repetitive templates.

The design agent should preserve the brand system while having enough creative freedom to find the strongest visual solution for each message.

Consistency should come primarily from:

- Logo behavior
- IBM Plex typography
- Black/white foundation
- Disciplined accent color
- Strong hierarchy
- Deliberate whitespace
- Clean composition
- Product relevance
- Restraint

Variation may come from:

- Layout
- Scale
- Accent color
- Visual metaphor
- Product/UI presentation
- Photography
- 3D treatment
- Graphic forms
- Campaign-specific art direction

## 7. Reference Philosophy

Reference designs are used to teach the studio **why good designs work**.

When studying another brand, separate transferable principles from brand-specific identity.

We may learn from:

- composition
- hierarchy
- whitespace
- typography
- pacing
- information density
- product presentation
- campaign systems
- visual storytelling

We should not copy:

- another brand's logo
- proprietary brand shapes
- distinctive identity assets
- exact compositions
- exact color systems merely because they appear in a reference

The goal is to build an original Abaad visual language informed by excellent design practice.

## 8. Current Non-Negotiables

1. The Abaad A symbol is both the icon and the letter A in the brand identity.
2. Black and white are the core visual foundation.
3. Additional colors are flexible accents used intentionally.
4. IBM Plex is the official typography family: IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic and IBM Plex Sans for Latin/numbers.
5. Designs should be clean, modern, strongly art-directed, and not unnecessarily crowded.
6. Reference brands inform design principles; they must not be copied.
7. The visual approach remains flexible so the strongest concept can determine whether a design uses typography, UI, imagery, 3D, graphics, or a combination.
